## Q06 [cause]: What was the Doctrine of Lapse and how did it relate to the revolt?

**Seed entities:** Doctrine of Lapse

**Answer:** The Doctrine of Lapse was a policy initiated by the East India Company, associated with James Broun-Ramsay, 1st Marquess of Dalhousie [1, 2]. It stipulated that any Indian princely state under the Company’s suzerainty would be annexed if the ruler was “manifestly incompetent or died without a male heir” [1, 2]. This policy supplanted the Indian sovereign’s right to choose a successor [1, 2].

The Doctrine of Lapse is directly linked to the revolt through its role in fueling grievances. Many nobles, such as Nana Sahib and Rani of Jhansi, rebelled because their legal and hereditary claims were rejected due to the Doctrine of Lapse [3]. The annexation of Jhansi itself was “caused by” the Doctrine of Lapse [1]. The controversy surrounding the “greased cartridge affair” further exacerbated tensions, and the British actions at Cawnpore, marked by summary violence, fueled a cycle of reprisals, with “Remember Cawnpore!” becoming a war cry [3, 4].
